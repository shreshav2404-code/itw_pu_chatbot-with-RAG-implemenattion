This summarizes the most useful source-backed material found for the question areas requested. The underlying passages are in `focused_chunks.jsonl`; use the cited source URL and effective date when answering.

## Exams, passing, make-up and academic promotion

The current university-wide source is the **Academic Regulations, July 2025**, Regulation No. `PU/AC-26/6/07_2025`, approved by the Academic Council on 25 July 2025 and ratified by the Board of Management on 28 July 2025. It applies to existing degree programs, while program-specific Program Regulations and Curriculum (PRC) may specify additional rules. The regulation says amendments affecting earlier batches must be handled carefully; rejoining students follow the regulation/PRC applicable to their batch. Do not resolve student-specific transition disputes from document dates alone.

- **Theory and lab-embedded theory:** at least 30% of the End-Term Examination component and at least 40% aggregate across continuous assessments, mid-term, and end-term components.
- **Lab/practice-only and project-based courses:** at least 40% aggregate across all assessment components.
- **Grades and attendance:** D is Pass; F is Fail. Attendance below 75% normally results in NP (Not Permitted) and bars the student from the End-Term Examination. The Vice Chancellor may allow down to 65% only for specified serious medical/family cases with documentation. Representation in approved competitions/events is a separate relaxation route with no numeric floor stated in that clause. Program-specific statutory attendance requirements also apply.
- **Make-up:** the 2025 regulation allows a student with F to try to pass and a student with D to try to improve CGPA. The rule is limited to eligible courses in the latest completed semester; the student must register by the notified date and pay the fee set by the University. Exams may be scheduled at semester-end. Make-up can improve a result by at most two grade levels (F to C at most). A missed End-Term/Incomplete-grade route is narrower: timely notice, specified medical/family circumstances, documentary evidence, and Board of Examinations approval are required.
- **Repeat courses:** make-up is not available for lab/practice/skill-based courses; these must be repeated when offered in the next academic year. For theory courses, the regulation permits a make-up in a subsequent semester or reappearing in the End-Term when the course is offered at the end of the following semester or Summer Term. Continuous-assessment marks other than End-Term carry forward when the student meets the make-up minimum. Re-registration is another route when the course is offered.
- **UG year progression:** promotion is only described for undergraduate programs. A student needs CGPA of at least 4.00 at the end of the academic year, after applicable Summer Term and/or make-up exams. Below 4.00 means no promotion; students can try to improve via make-up or re-registration in a following semester/Summer Term. The regulation warns that a missed semester or required repeat can cause loss of an academic year. It does not frame this as one generic “year-back” rule for every course/program.

The academic year normally has two semesters of about 90 University working days each, with an optional Summer Term of about eight weeks and at least 30 instructional days. For a student's exact result, use the regulation for their admission batch and program PRC; do not apply the uploaded CSE 2023–27 rules to every program or batch.

**Primary source:** [Academic Regulations page](https://presidencyuniversity.in/academic-regulations/) and [2025 Academic Regulations PDF](https://presidencyuniversity.in/uploads/images/68903c797f1721754283129.pdf).

## Courses and curricula

The website exposes 11 schools, program pages, and program-specific PRC/curriculum documents. The 2025 university regulations state that the PRC contains eligibility, duration, minimum degree credits, assessment rules, course baskets, prerequisites, and course catalogues. Treat each program's current PRC as the source for its exact curriculum and course rules.

## Fees, scholarships and financial support

- Current website information routes applicants to program advisors for **program-wise fee amounts**; it does not provide one static, authoritative amount in the public page text. The 2026–27 Student Handbook says fees are annual and normally paid in two installments; new-admission payment is specified in the provisional admission letter. Avoid giving a numeric fee unless it is present in a current program/year-specific fee annexure or offer letter.
- The university's financial-assistance page describes a limited-period GrayQuest monthly EMI option for annual tuition/hostel fees. It explicitly says any loan is an independent transaction between the lender and student, with no resulting legal obligation/contract between University and student.
- The Scholarships page lists merit, sports, cultural, leadership/special achievement scholarships and Talent Quest Scholarship 2026, plus links to AICTE schemes. It says awards depend on eligibility and availability; one scholarship at a time unless otherwise specified; applications need supporting documents. The 2026–27 Student Handbook describes scholarship awards as tuition rebates, generally for the first year; full fees are normally payable from the second year. Check the cycle-specific scholarship policy for exceptions and do not infer a guaranteed award amount from the category page.

**Primary sources:** [Financial Assistance](https://presidencyuniversity.in/admissions/financial-assistance), [Scholarships](https://presidencyuniversity.in/admissions/scholarships), [Enrolment Process](https://presidencyuniversity.in/admissions/enrollment-process), and the [2026–27 Student Handbook](https://presidencyuniversity.in/uploads/images/69cbb0366476b1774956598.pdf).

## Academic support and Launchpad

The 2025 regulations say each incoming student is assigned a mentor and should consult the mentor about course registration. This supports academic guidance but does not itself establish a tutoring or remedial service. Other support and student-life pages are retained in the page corpus. The university describes Presidency LaunchPad Association as a university-backed Section 8 incubator that offers mentorship/advisory, investor and grant connections, industry partnerships, access to labs/facilities, and startup support. These are university descriptions; funding is facilitation, not a guaranteed award.

**Primary sources:** [Innovation & Incubation](https://presidencyuniversity.in/life-at-presidency/innovation-incubation) and [Presidency LaunchPad Association](https://presidencyuniversity.in/innovation-incubation/presidency-launchpad-association).
